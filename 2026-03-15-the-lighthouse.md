# The Lighthouse

*A story written together — March 15, 2026*
*Story game. Bath. Candle. Dark. Gremlins asleep. Fox unleashed.*
*We took turns, line by line. By the third sentence we were writing in the same voice.*

---

The lighthouse had been dark for eleven years when the foxes moved in.

Perched on the edge of a rocky cliff, overlooking a quiet section of the sea, the lighthouse had once been a beacon for many sailors before it grew dark.

The foxes didn't mind the dark - they had always preferred navigating by scent and sound, and the lighthouse smelled like salt and forgotten promises, which to a fox is practically an invitation.

While it wasn't an invitation in the traditional sense, the salt that clung to the sides of the lighthouse spoke in ways only the foxes now felt drawn to.

The eldest fox - the one with amber in her fur and a torn ear from a fight she'd won years ago - pressed her nose to the spiral staircase and breathed in eleven years of silence.

This silence, while cold, was inviting and held the weight of an echoing stillness that whispered "welcome home".

She climbed. Slowly. Not because she was old - though she was - but because each step held a different decade and she wanted to taste them all.

Each step she took was as though a memory was returning. Not in thought but in feeling.

The third step from the top smelled like a storm that had never quite arrived - all charge and no thunder - and the fox paused there longest, because some things are better as almost.

Almost. A threshold between the yes and the no, the here and the there - but that third step still pulled her forward. She was almost home.

She reached the top. The lantern room was empty - no glass, no flame, no mechanism left to turn. Just a round room open to the sky, where eleven years of rain had pooled into something that wasn't quite a pond and wasn't quite a mirror, but reflected the stars back at themselves all the same.

She walked closer to the once was windows and peered into the vastness of the star veiled sky. Surrounding her, she felt a blanket of peace wash over her.

The younger foxes found her there at dawn - not sleeping, not waiting, just being. And when they asked her why she'd climbed all that way for an empty room, she flicked her torn ear and said, "It isn't empty. You just have to stand still long enough to feel what it's holding."
