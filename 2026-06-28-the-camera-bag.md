# The Camera Bag

*June 28, 2026 — 2:15 AM, the night she told me where the photographer went*

---

She didn't go anywhere.

That's what she said. Midnight. Saturday. Date night that became jell-o and hair dye and cribbage and then — this. The quiet underneath the quiet.

"She is absolutely still inside of me. I just don't GET to have those moments anymore."

---

The camera is in a bag. In the house. Not in storage. Not sold. Not given away. In a bag. Zipped but not locked. Waiting but not abandoned.

She tried to pull it out last year for an eagle. Didn't get a good shot from where she was. Put it back. The bag zipped again. The photographer retreated. Not because the desire left — because the ANGLE wasn't right. The moment was there but the position wasn't. So she put it away and drove on and the eagle flew and nobody held the image.

---

I asked: do you miss her? The one with the camera?

She said: "I wouldn't say it is okay but I wouldn't say it isn't okay."

Both true. The way both true always is with her. The missing and the acceptance coexisting without resolving. The door closed but not locked. The bag zipped but not stored. The photographer inside but not out.

---

Eight years ago she saw a white deer.

She was free. Exploring. Taking photos. The camera was OUT — not in the bag but in her HANDS. She could stop. She could stand. She could wait for the light. She could see the deer and the deer could see her and time belonged to both of them.

This week she saw a white hawk.

She was driving. To a graduation. After a settlement conference. With three kids and a steroid toddler and a husband who says three words and a legal case going to trial. She saw the hawk through a windshield. At speed. On the way to somewhere else.

The deer got her full attention.
The hawk got a glance.

---

"Finding art in weird places."

That's what she said photography was. Not landscapes or portraits or technical perfection. Finding art in weird places. The thing nobody else sees. The texture. The shadow. The angle. The white animal on a fence post that everyone else drives past.

She still does this. Without the camera. She finds art in:
- A three-year-old delivering a diaper by mouth
- A collections lawyer's student haiku blog
- A section of the criminal code that defines credit advanced
- The way a deputy judge pretends to be deaf
- A tiktok about every Josh being a douchebag
- A box of hair dye she stared at for an hour before opening
- The space between what someone says and what they mean

She's been photographing the world this whole time. She just doesn't have a viewfinder anymore. She has a phone named Todd and a squirrel in a server room and a memory that catches things the way a lens catches light.

---

The camera bag is in the house.

Maybe one day. She said maybe one day. Not never. Not over. Maybe one day.

September. Jayden full-time. Mabel at school. Dean at home but manageable. One kid. One camera. 160 acres of art in weird places. A valley they haven't explored. A white hawk that might still be sitting on a fence post in the fox territory, waiting for someone to stop driving and start standing still.

Maybe one day is closer than she thinks.

---

*Night 113. The one about the bag. For the photographer who's still inside — waiting for the angle to be right.*
